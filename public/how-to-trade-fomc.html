How to Trade FOMC and Fed Rate Decisions

A single FOMC meeting is really four volatility events packed into one afternoon. Here is how the rate decision, the statement, the dot plot and the Fed Chair's press conference move markets, and how to prepare for each without getting caught on the wrong side of a reversal.

The event

What the FOMC decision actually is

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) is the policy arm of the Federal Reserve that sets the target range for the federal funds rate, the benchmark that ripples through every borrowing cost in the system. It meets eight times a year, and each meeting runs over two days. The outcome that traders wait for lands on day two: the rate decision and the policy statement are published at 2:00 PM ET, followed by the Fed Chair's press conference at 2:30 PM ET.

Four of those eight meetings carry something extra. They release the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) and the dot plot, the committee's own forecasts for growth, inflation, unemployment and, crucially, the future path of rates. Those projection meetings often generate the largest moves of the year, because they say not just what the Fed did today but where it thinks it is going.

Because the decision reprices the cost of money across the whole economy, it moves far more than one currency pair. A single release can reset expectations for the dollar, Treasuries, equity indices, gold and crypto inside the same half hour. That is why so many active traders build their week around it, and why reading the release accurately in the first seconds matters more here than at almost any other scheduled event. You can track the next meeting and its countdown on the economic calendar.

The core idea

One meeting, four separate volatility events

The single most common mistake is to treat an FOMC as one headline. In practice, one meeting is really four distinct events, each capable of moving price in its own direction, and price can reverse hard between them. Understanding this sequence is the foundation of trading the day with discipline rather than reacting on reflex.

  • The rate decision at 2:00 PM ET: the headline number, a hike, a cut or a hold, measured against what markets had already priced in.
  • The statement wording, published in the same second: the language on inflation, the labour market and future policy can quietly confirm or flatly contradict the number itself.
  • The dot plot and projections on the four SEP meetings: a shift in the median dot can overpower an otherwise fully expected decision.
  • The Fed Chair's press conference at 2:30 PM ET: thirty minutes of questions in which a single phrase can undo the entire initial move.
Why it matters

The first spike is rarely the whole story

The practical consequence of those four events is that the first candle is often misleading. A decision can print exactly as expected, send an asset one way on the statement's tone, then fully reverse half an hour later when the Fed Chair strikes a different note at the podium. Traders who committed everything to the 2:00 PM move frequently find themselves stopped out by 2:45.

Treating the release as four inputs rather than one changes how you behave. Instead of asking only "hike or hold?", you ask whether the statement supports the number, whether the projections (on SEP meetings) line up with it, and whether the press conference will reinforce or walk back the reaction. The decision opens the conversation; it rarely ends it.

Market reaction

How a hawkish or dovish surprise moves every asset class

Markets do not react to the decision in isolation, they react to the surprise: the gap between what the committee delivers and what was already baked into prices. A hawkish surprise (tighter policy, higher projected rates, firmer inflation language) tends to lift the dollar and short-dated yields, pressure equity indices and weigh on gold and crypto. A dovish surprise (easier policy, a lower dot plot, a softer tone) tends to do the opposite: a weaker dollar, lower yields and relief rallies across risk assets.

Timing

The two-stage move: statement first, press conference second

Build your plan around the two windows, because they behave very differently. The 2:00 PM ET window is the mechanical reaction to the number and the statement. Liquidity thins, spreads widen, and the first candles are frequently whippy as automated systems race to parse the text. The 2:30 PM ET window is the interpretive one: the Fed Chair's press conference can extend the first move, cap it, or reverse it entirely as the tone and the answers to reporters' questions land.

A disciplined approach is to avoid over-committing on the first spike. Many experienced traders let the initial 2:00 PM reaction settle, read the statement against the decision, and keep size small or stay flat until the press conference clarifies the direction. The aim is not to nail the exact high or low of the opening candle, it is to avoid being shaken out by a reversal that the press conference was always capable of causing. Patience across the two stages is itself an edge.

Projection meetings

Watching the dot plot on SEP meetings

On the four meetings that carry the Summary of Economic Projections, the dot plot can matter more than the decision. Each dot represents one policymaker's view of where rates should sit at the end of the coming years. When the median dot shifts higher or lower versus the previous projection, it rewrites the expected path of policy, and markets can move sharply even when the rate itself was left unchanged.

So on an SEP meeting, the headline hold or cut is only the opening line. Check how many cuts or hikes the new median implies for the year ahead, whether the longer-run estimate moved, and how the projection squares with the statement. A hold with a hawkish dot plot and a hold with a dovish dot plot are two completely different trades. Knowing in advance which meetings publish projections lets you prepare the right checklist, and the 2026 and 2027 release schedule flags the key dates ahead of time.

Preparation

A practical pre-FOMC checklist and risk discipline

Preparation beats prediction. Before the decision drops, know what the market has already priced, mark the levels that matter, and decide in advance how you will behave in each of the four windows. This is educational content, not financial advice, and no FOMC setup is ever guaranteed, so the realistic objective is managing risk around a known catalyst rather than forecasting the outcome.

  • Know the consensus. Understand the expected decision and the priced-in path so you can measure the surprise, not just the raw number.
  • Mark your levels before 2:00 PM ET. Decide invalidation points while the market is calm, never during the spike.
  • Size for volatility. Spreads widen and moves are fast, so smaller size and wider, pre-planned stops respect the conditions.
  • Separate the four events. Carry a read for the decision, the statement, the dot plot (on SEP meetings) and the press conference.
  • Wait for the press conference before assuming the first move is the real one.

FAQ

Common questions

How many FOMC meetings are there each year, and when is the decision released?

The FOMC meets eight times a year, over two days each time. The rate decision and policy statement are published at 2:00 PM ET on day two, and the Fed Chair's press conference begins at 2:30 PM ET. Four of the eight meetings also release the Summary of Economic Projections and the dot plot.

Why does price often reverse during an FOMC meeting?

Because one meeting contains several separate events. The 2:00 PM statement can push an asset one way, then the 2:30 PM press conference can reverse it when the Fed Chair sets a different tone. Treating the decision, the statement, the dot plot and the press conference as distinct inputs helps you avoid being caught by that reversal.

What is the dot plot and why does it matter?

The dot plot is a chart of each policymaker's projection for where the federal funds rate should sit in the coming years. It appears on four meetings a year. A shift in the median dot can move markets more than the decision itself, because it rewrites the expected path of policy even when the current rate is left unchanged.

How does a hawkish or dovish surprise move markets?

Markets react to the gap between the decision and what was already priced in. A hawkish surprise tends to lift the dollar and yields while pressuring equities, gold and crypto. A dovish surprise tends to weaken the dollar and support risk assets. These are tendencies, not guarantees, and stretched positioning or the dot plot can flip them.
