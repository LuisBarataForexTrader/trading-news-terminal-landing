Futures markets: the professional trader's instrument

Futures contracts are standardised legal agreements to buy or sell a specific asset at a predetermined price on a future date. They trade on regulated exchanges (CME, ICE, EUREX, CBOT) and provide traders with leverage, liquidity and price discovery across equities, bonds, currencies, commodities and crypto. The CME Group alone facilitates over $1 trillion in notional value per day.

Unlike stocks, futures markets open during pre-market and after-hours sessions — making them the primary vehicle for immediate price discovery when major news breaks outside regular equity market hours. The S&P 500 E-mini (ES) and Nasdaq 100 E-mini (NQ) futures tell you where equities are likely to open before the NYSE or Nasdaq bells ring.

The most important futures contracts for news traders

News-driven traders should understand the leading futures contracts in each asset class:

  • E-mini S&P 500 (ES, CME): The flagship US equity futures contract. Every US economic release moves ES in real time. Trades nearly 24 hours — perfect for overnight news event positioning. One point = $50 per contract.
  • E-mini Nasdaq 100 (NQ, CME): Higher beta than ES. More sensitive to tech earnings, Fed rate expectations and risk-on/risk-off. Two times the volatility of ES in major macro events.
  • Euro Stoxx 50 (FESX, EUREX): European equity benchmark futures. ECB decisions, eurozone PMI and German Ifo are primary catalysts. Trades 08:00–22:00 CET.
  • US Treasury futures (ZN, ZB, CME): 10-year Note (ZN) and 30-year Bond (ZB) futures. Immediately react to Fed news, CPI, NFP. Key for fixed income and equity correlation traders.
  • Crude Oil futures (CL, NYMEX): WTI benchmark. Highest-volume commodity future. OPEC meetings, EIA inventory data, geopolitical events are primary catalysts.
  • Gold futures (GC, COMEX): 100 troy oz per contract. Directly linked to real rates, USD and safe-haven demand. Reacts to virtually every major macro data release.

Futures and pre-market price discovery

One of the most important functions of futures markets for active traders is pre-market price discovery. When major news breaks outside regular market hours:

  • After-hours earnings releases (Apple, Google, Amazon) immediately reprice NQ futures — showing traders where the Nasdaq 100 will open the next morning.
  • US economic data (NFP at 08:30 ET, CPI at 08:30 ET) moves ES, NQ and ZN futures before equity markets open at 09:30 ET.
  • Geopolitical events overnight cause ES to gap at the CME open (18:00 ET) — the first price signal for the following day.

Commitment of Traders (COT) report

Every Friday, the CFTC publishes the Commitment of Traders report — showing the aggregate futures positions of commercial hedgers, large speculators (managed money) and small traders. Extreme speculative positioning (net long or net short at historical extremes) can signal potential reversal setups when catalysed by news. Widely followed by macro hedge funds and commodity traders.
