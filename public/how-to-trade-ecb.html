How to Trade the

Eight times a year the ECB Governing Council sets euro area rates. Here is how the decision, the quarterly projections and the press conference move markets, and how to walk in with a plan instead of a guess.

The event

What the ECB Governing Council decides

The European Central Bank sets monetary policy for the euro area through its Governing Council, which meets eight times a year, roughly every six weeks. Each meeting runs across two days. The decision itself, together with the accompanying monetary policy statement, is published at 2:15 PM CET on the second day. Forty-five minutes later, at 2:45 PM CET, the ECB President opens a press conference that is often more market moving than the rate number alone.

The rate traders watch most closely is the deposit facility rate, the rate the central bank pays on reserves that commercial banks park with it overnight. It has become the effective policy anchor for the euro area, so a change there, or a signal that one is coming, feeds straight into funding markets, the euro and European equities. When the decision matches what markets already expect, the first reaction can be muted. When it surprises, the move is immediate.

You can confirm the exact date and time of the next meeting on the economic calendar, which runs a live countdown to the release so you are never caught off guard by the clock.

The volatility multiplier

Why staff projections turn a quiet meeting loud

Four of the ECB's eight meetings carry an extra ingredient: the quarterly staff macroeconomic projections, published in March, June, September and December. These forecasts cover growth and inflation several years out, and they frame how the Governing Council sees the path of policy from here.

For traders, projection meetings behave much like the Fed's dot plot meetings. The raw decision can be a non event, yet a small revision to the inflation forecast can move the euro sharply because it reshapes expectations for the next move. Knowing in advance whether a meeting is a projection meeting tells you how much volatility to budget for, and whether to expect a clean one-stage reaction or a messy repricing. The release dates for 2026 and 2027 make it easy to map which meetings carry projections and which do not.

The mechanics

The two-stage move: statement, then press conference

ECB day almost always plays out in two distinct stages, and the gap between them is where many traders get caught.

Stage one is the 2:15 PM CET statement. The market reads the rate decision and the written language, and price reacts within seconds. Stage two is the 2:45 PM CET press conference, where the ECB President explains the reasoning, answers questions and, crucially, delivers forward guidance on what might come next. The tone of that press conference can confirm the first move or reverse it entirely. A decision that initially looked hawkish can soften when the President stresses downside risks, and a dovish first read can flip when the President sounds in no hurry to cut.

This is why experienced traders treat the statement and the press conference as one event with two acts. Reacting hard to the 2:15 print and ignoring the 2:45 commentary is one of the most common ways to be on the right side of the headline and the wrong side of the close.

The ripple

How the decision moves EUR pairs, indices and bonds

Because the euro is one of the most traded currencies in the world, an ECB decision sends ripples across several asset classes at once. A single statement can reprice currencies, equities and sovereign debt in the same minute.

Where the ECB decision typically shows up first
AssetWhat typically reacts
EUR pairsEUR/USD leads the move, with EUR/GBP and EUR/JPY amplifying it when the euro trends against a weaker or stronger counterpart
European indicesThe DAX, CAC 40 and Euro Stoxx 50 respond to the rate path and to the tone on growth and financing conditions
European bondsGerman Bund yields move on the policy signal, while periphery spreads (Italian and Spanish yields over Germany) widen or narrow on risk sentiment
The periphery read

Watch the spread, not just the headline rate

Periphery spreads deserve special attention. When the ECB sounds willing to support the bloc, the gap between Italian or Spanish yields and German Bund yields tends to narrow. When it sounds restrictive, or when fragmentation fears rise, that gap can widen quickly, and it often leads the euro rather than lagging it.

Watching the spread gives a cleaner read on how the market is interpreting the decision than the EUR/USD candle alone. A euro that rallies while periphery spreads widen is sending a mixed message, and that divergence is frequently an early warning that the first reaction will not hold through the press conference.

The preparation

How to prepare for ECB day

You cannot predict the exact wording of a statement, but you can prepare so that you are reacting from a plan rather than from surprise.

  • Know the consensus. Write down the expected decision and the expected tone before the event, so you can measure the actual surprise against it rather than guessing in the moment.
  • Read the forward guidance, not just the number. The path signalled for coming meetings usually matters more to price than the current rate.
  • Do not over-commit on the first spike. The 2:15 move can reverse during the 2:45 press conference, so loading up into the initial candle leaves no room for the second stage.
  • Size for two-way risk. Position sizing that survives a sharp reversal matters more on ECB day than on a quiet session, because spreads widen and stops can be run.
  • Have a plan for doing nothing. If the decision and the tone both land as expected, the cleanest option is often to stand aside.

FAQ

Common questions

What time is the ECB rate decision released?

The monetary policy decision and statement are published at 2:15 PM CET on the second day of the Governing Council meeting. The ECB President's press conference follows at 2:45 PM CET. Both times matter, because the press conference can move markets more than the decision itself.

Which ECB rate should traders watch?

The deposit facility rate is the effective policy anchor for the euro area, so it is the rate most traders track. A change there, or guidance that one is coming, feeds directly into the euro, European bonds and equities.

Why does the euro sometimes reverse after the ECB decision?

Because ECB day has two stages. The 2:15 CET statement drives the first move, then the 2:45 CET press conference can confirm or reverse it as the ECB President delivers forward guidance. A hawkish first read can fade if the tone turns cautious, and a dovish read can flip the other way.

How do ECB staff projections affect volatility?

Four meetings a year (March, June, September and December) include quarterly staff macroeconomic projections. Much like the Fed's dot plot, a revision to the growth or inflation forecast can move the euro sharply even when the rate itself is unchanged, so projection meetings usually carry more volatility.
